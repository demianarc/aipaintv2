How it works

Three quiet steps.

  1. 01

    A painting is pulled at random

    From The Met's Open Access program — only public-domain works. Use the filters to narrow by century, movement, culture, or medium.

  2. 02

    It's interpreted in seconds

    The image and catalog data are sent to a multimodal model that looks at the canvas and writes a short, contextual reading — what's there, and what it might mean.

  3. 03

    You read, listen, save, repeat

    Stream-read or audio-listen. Save to favorites with one tap. Hit N for the next painting, F to favorite.

A taste

What an interpretation looks like.

From the collection

Wheat Field with Cypresses

Vincent van Gogh · 1889

Van Gogh paints the wind the way a child draws breath — too much, too fast, all at once.

Notice how the cypresses on the right answer the cloud-banks above: a private grief and a public weather, both reaching into the same sky. The wheat doesn't sway — it churns, each stalk a small typhoon of paint, as if the field itself were unable to hold still. The Alpilles in the distance are impossibly calm by comparison, a single geological note in a landscape that has, for a moment, forgotten its manners.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is this really free? What's the catch?

It's free. There's no catch and no account. Compute is paid out of pocket; if it ever stops being sustainable I'll say so on the about page. No ads, no tracking, no email harvesting.

How accurate is the AI's interpretation?

It's an interpretation, not gospel. The model can be confidently wrong about provenance, dates, or symbolism. Treat it the way you'd treat a smart, well-read friend at the museum: a starting point for your own looking, not the final word.

Where do the paintings come from?

The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Open Access program — about half a million public-domain works across European, American, and Asian collections. Only works marked "public domain" are shown.

Can I save what I find?

Yes — tap the heart on any painting and it lands on your Favorites page. Favorites are stored in your browser's local storage; nothing leaves your device.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. The app is built mobile-first. The audio narration in particular is the nicest way to use it — slip it into your pocket while you walk.

What model is doing the interpretation?

OpenAI's GPT-4o family for vision and text, ElevenLabs for the voice. Models are swapped as better ones land — the prompt is the slow-changing part.

Ready to see something?

One painting. One reading. Sixty seconds. No account, no email, no payment.
